The case for a slower, wetter walk to work

Walking in the rain is better for you than it feels, according to commuters who tried it.

irtBy Irtnews staff24 November 2026 · 3 min read

When the forecast turns grey, the first thing many commuters do is give up on walking. But people who keep going through the wet months say the habit is worth it, and doctors agree that regular walking is one of the simplest ways to stay healthy.

A brisk walk raises the heart rate, strengthens muscles and gives a chance to clear your head before the working day. Daylight, even on a cloudy morning, also helps set the body clock, which can make it easier to sleep at night.

How to make it bearable

The trick, regular walkers say, is the right kit. A light waterproof jacket, shoes with good grip and a spare pair of socks in your bag make a big difference. Some people keep office shoes at work so they never have to sit in damp ones.

The rain is never as bad as it looks from the window.

Start small

If a full walk to work is too far, try getting off the bus a stop early or walking the last part of the journey. Small changes add up over a week, and many people find they miss the walk on days they skip it.

As always, take care on slippery pavements, wear something bright in the dark and choose well-lit routes.
