The indie bookshops winning back high streets

Reading clubs, late openings and a cat in the window are part of the formula.

irtBy Irtnews staff24 November 2026 · 4 min read

Not long ago, independent bookshops were seen as a dying trade. Now many are opening new branches, and some are becoming the busiest shops on their streets.

Owners say the key is offering something online stores cannot: a place to browse, meet people and get a recommendation from someone who has read the book.

More than shops

Many hold reading groups, children's story times and author talks. Some open late one evening a week with a small café or bar. A few have a resident cat, which owners say is very good for business.

People want to be surprised by a book they did not know they were looking for.

The challenges

Margins on books are thin, and rents on busy streets are high. Many shops rely on loyal customers ordering through them rather than online. Some have started subscription boxes, sending a hand-picked book each month.

Booksellers say local councils could help by keeping business rates fair and supporting events that bring people into town centres.
