Inside the chip plants that never switch off

Engineers work in shifts around a production line where one speck of dust can ruin a batch.

irtBy Irtnews staff24 November 2026 · 5 min readShare on X

From the outside, a chip factory looks like any large warehouse. Inside, it is one of the cleanest places on Earth. Air is filtered constantly, workers wear full-body suits and the lighting is often tinted yellow to protect light-sensitive materials used in production.

The machines inside run day and night. Stopping a production line and starting it again is so costly that plants are designed never to switch off, except for planned maintenance.

Hundreds of steps

Turning a disc of silicon into finished chips can take weeks and involve hundreds of separate steps: coating, exposing patterns with light, etching, cleaning and testing. A single particle in the wrong place can ruin a whole wafer.

The product is invisible to the eye, so every check is done by machines that measure in billionths of a metre.

The people behind it

Despite the automation, the plants depend on skilled people. Engineers monitor equipment, technicians swap parts and specialists study tiny defects to find out where they came from. Many plants say finding enough trained staff is now harder than buying new machines.

Training schemes with local colleges are growing, offering paid apprenticeships that lead straight into cleanroom jobs.

Why it matters

Chips sit inside almost everything, from cars to fridges. Governments in several regions are offering money to build new plants closer to home after recent shortages showed how fragile supply chains can be. Each new plant takes years to build, so the decisions being made now will shape supply for the next decade.
