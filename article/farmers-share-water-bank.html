Water

Farmers along a drying river agree to share a water bank

Growers in three regions will pool reservoir rights and trade them through a single ledger.

irtBy Irtnews staff24 November 2026 · 4 min read

After three dry summers in a row, farmers along a shrinking river have agreed to try something new. Instead of each farm guarding its own share of reservoir water, they will pool their rights in a shared water bank.

Under the plan, farmers who do not need their full allowance in a given month can lend it to neighbours who do. Every transfer will be recorded in a single public ledger so that everyone can see where the water goes.

Why now

Farm groups say the old system encouraged waste. Growers worried that if they did not use their full share, they might lose it the following year. As a result, water was sometimes used on crops that did not need it while other farms went short.

Water that sits unused on one farm can save a harvest on the next.

How it will work

Each farm keeps its legal right to water, but can choose to put part of it into the bank. A small committee of farmers and water officials will set the price for borrowing and decide what happens in emergencies.

Environmental groups have welcomed the plan but want a guaranteed amount left in the river for fish and wildlife. Organisers say that amount will be fixed before the scheme starts in the spring.
