Cities race to cool their streets before next summer

Shade trees, pale roofs and water fountains top the list as councils plan for longer heatwaves.

irtBy Irtnews staff24 November 2026 · 4 min readShare on X

Planning offices are not usually busy in late November, but in many cities the work on next summer has already begun. Councils are drawing up lists of streets that become dangerously hot in a heatwave and deciding what can be changed before June.

The quickest fixes are often the simplest. Trees planted along bus routes give shade within a few seasons. Light-coloured paint on roofs and pavements reflects sunlight that dark surfaces would otherwise soak up and release at night.

Mapping the hot spots

Many cities now use thermal maps taken from planes or satellites to find the worst blocks. The pattern is consistent: wide roads, few trees and large car parks run several degrees hotter than leafy streets a short walk away.

The hottest streets are rarely a surprise to the people who live on them.

Residents in those areas are being asked where they would most like shade, drinking fountains and benches. Some councils are turning school playgrounds into cool parks that stay open during the summer holidays.

The cost question

Planting and caring for a street tree costs money for years, not just on the day it goes in the ground. Officials say the investment pays back through lower energy use, fewer heat-related hospital visits and streets that people actually want to walk along.

The bigger changes, such as replacing car parks with green squares, will take longer. But the message from planners is that every summer of delay makes the work more urgent.
